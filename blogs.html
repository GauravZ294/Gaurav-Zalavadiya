🧠 What Is Testing?

Thoughts on how testing quietly appears in everyday decisions—not only in software.

Definition.

Here I’m trying to define testing—but honestly, I don’t even know what I’m saying about it. And maybe that’s the point. Right now, I’m testing whether this definition makes sense, whether it connects with someone—or not. This itself is a form of testing. I’m testing if my definition becomes popular, or if people even understand what I mean.

The Truth Is:

Testing is a core part of life. In every phase, you’re constantly testing—whether something works or fails. And you probably don’t even realize how many times a day you perform a “test” in your routine life.

Testing always gives two results: pass or fail. That’s it. Our basic decision-making and everyday actions often boil down to this simple logic.

Let Me Ask You Something:

Who are you? A student, teacher, doctor, police officer, worker, businessman, employee, or something else? No matter your role, in every position or stage of life, you’re running tests—consciously or unconsciously.

This Blog Is Raw:

This blog is written in my own way—maybe not professional, but it’s real. It’s from my mindset, and I don’t know if you’ll like it. But I didn’t overthink it. I just wrote it as it came to me.

So, can I say that according to my point of view, I passed—but according to your view, maybe I failed? That’s also testing. That’s also life.

Bottom Line:

Whenever you get two results in specific conditions—like an if-else situation—that’s testing. Testing is not something that’s born or fixed; it’s something that appears whenever results depend on a pass or fail. That’s when testing is born—even if temporarily.
